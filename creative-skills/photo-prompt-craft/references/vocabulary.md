# Photography vocabulary

Use this lexicon in every photographic prompt. Generic words like "good lighting" or "nice background" produce generic output; specific photographic terms shift any image generator toward commercial quality.

Contents:
- Core lists (verbatim): lighting direction and quality, color temperature, lens and camera, surfaces and textures, shadow behavior, palette descriptors, atmosphere, quality markers, words to avoid
- Lighting setups by scenario (verbatim, collected from the product format guides)
- Supplement: camera angles and framing, film and photographic looks, color grading

## Lighting direction and quality

**Direction (always specify):**
- `45° camera-left` / `45° camera-right` — classic three-quarter key
- `frontal flat` — beauty / e-commerce flat fill
- `top-down beauty dish` — overhead beauty light
- `three-quarter back rim` — separation light from behind
- `side raking` — texture-revealing low side light
- `butterfly under-chin` — Hollywood / glamour light
- `Rembrandt 45° high` — classic portrait triangular shadow
- `under-camera fill` — secondary fill from below

**Quality (always specify):**
- `large softbox diffusion` — broad soft light source
- `hard direct strobe` — sharp-edged shadows
- `gobo-shaped` — controlled spill via flag
- `gridded spot` — narrow controlled beam
- `bounced fill` — soft secondary light off reflector
- `ambient skylight` — even diffused daylight
- `dappled foliage shadow` — natural broken pattern
- `motivated practical light` — light source visible in scene

## Color temperature (always specify in Kelvin)

| Setting | Kelvin | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| Candlelight | 2000–2800K | Warm intimate |
| Tungsten interior | 3000–3400K | Cozy domestic |
| Mixed warm interior | 3500–4000K | Lived-in residential |
| Fluorescent / mixed | 4000–4500K | Neutral interior |
| Daylight neutral | 5000–5500K | Studio standard |
| Overcast daylight | 6000–6500K | Cool soft outdoor |
| Blue hour / shade | 7000K+ | Cool dramatic |

Always cite a specific value, e.g. "4500K mixed window-and-tungsten" — not "warm light".

## Lens and camera specs

**Focal length (always specify):**
- `24mm wide` — environmental, immersive, slight distortion
- `35mm natural` — documentary, lifestyle, general purpose
- `50mm standard` — natural human eye, neutral
- `85mm portrait` — flattering compression, headshot standard
- `100mm macro` — extreme close detail
- `135mm telephoto` — strong subject isolation

**Aperture (always specify):**
- `f/1.4` — extreme shallow DoF, dreamy bokeh
- `f/1.8 to f/2` — shallow DoF, portrait separation
- `f/2.8` — moderate shallow, classic portrait
- `f/4 to f/5.6` — controlled DoF, both subject and context sharp-ish
- `f/8 to f/11` — deep DoF, everything sharp
- `f/16+` — landscape deep, all in focus

**Depth of field language:**
- `shallow depth of field with creamy bokeh background`
- `medium depth of field separating subject from environment`
- `deep depth of field with everything from foreground to background sharp`

## Surface and texture vocabulary

**Surfaces:**
- `seamless paper backdrop` — gradient-friendly studio
- `linen fabric texture` — warm, organic, tactile
- `raw oak / walnut wood grain` — natural, hand-crafted
- `polished marble` — premium, luxury, cold
- `concrete brutalist` — modern, raw, monolithic
- `brushed metal` — industrial, modern, cool
- `velvet pile` — luxurious, deep color
- `worn leather` — heritage, masculine, lived-in
- `aged paper` — vintage, scholarly, warm
- `ceramic glazed` — clean, minimal, crafted
- `woven jute / sisal` — boho, natural, textural

**Texture qualifiers:**
- `tack-sharp surface detail`
- `micro-pore visibility on skin`
- `individual hair strand definition`
- `condensation droplets on glass`
- `subtle dust catching light`
- `realistic fabric weave`

## Shadow behavior

Always specify how shadows fall:

- `soft falloff with gradient transition`
- `sharp-edged shadow with hard light source`
- `contact shadow only at base, no shadow on backdrop`
- `dappled broken shadow from foliage`
- `deep shadow side with selective fill`
- `gentle wraparound from large diffused source`

## Color palette descriptors

When describing palette, always be specific:

- `dominant warm earth — terracotta, ochre, dried sage`
- `cool neutral — bone white, soft grey, deep navy`
- `monochromatic deep green range from forest to moss`
- `warm cream against deep oxblood accent`
- `pastel trio — dusty rose, sage, butter cream`
- `high-contrast black with single saturated red accent`

## Atmosphere modifiers

These add cinematic depth:

- `shaft of light through dust motes`
- `gentle steam rising from coffee`
- `soft window light through sheer curtain`
- `golden hour backlight with anamorphic flare`
- `morning haze with light streaking through`
- `candlelight under-fill with warm bias`
- `cool overcast with even soft tones`

## Quality marker phrases

Append at the end of every prompt:

- `magazine-cover quality`
- `tack-sharp commercial photography`
- `hyper-detailed photorealistic rendering`
- `editorial commercial standard`
- `ultra-realistic textures and materials`
- `professional retouching standard`
- `no AI artifacts`

## Words to avoid in prompts

These are too vague and produce mediocre output:

- Avoid "good lighting" -> use: specify direction, quality, Kelvin
- Avoid "nice background" -> use: specify surface, color, gradient
- Avoid "beautiful product" -> use: specify hero shot, angle, composition
- Avoid "professional photo" -> use: describe the exact commercial register and specify lens
- Avoid "high quality" -> use: specify tack-sharp, hyper-detailed
- Avoid "realistic" -> use: specify hyper-realistic, no AI artifacts
- Avoid "modern style" -> use: describe the concrete palette, materials, and composition

## Lighting setups by scenario

Verbatim setups from the product format guides. Each states direction, quality and Kelvin; reuse them as complete lighting sentences.

### Beauty and closeup with hands or skin
- serum-application: Soft beauty dish key from camera-front, slight under-fill, 5000K, gentle catchlight on glass and skin
- lipstick-on-lips / eye-makeup: Beauty dish or ring light frontal, 5500K, sharp catchlight on lips/eye, even skin tone
- hand-holding-vertical: Window light camera-side, 4500K, rim separation behind product, soft fill on hand
- pour-into-palm: Top-down softbox key, 5000K, sharp falloff highlighting liquid texture, hand in shadow
- dropper-mid-air: Hard directional key with grid, 5000K, freezes droplet in sharp focus, deep shadow background
- hands-cradling-jar: Soft warm window light, 3500K, intimate ritual feel, gentle highlight on jar rim
- texture-on-skin: Side raking light to reveal texture, 5000K, sharp shadow detail in product surface
- face-touch-product: Soft front-fill with key 45° camera-left, 4500K, glowing skin highlight
- mouth-bite-or-sip: Practical warm light or window key, 4000K, appetizing highlight on product

### Fashion and on-model
- studio-clean: Large softbox key 45° camera-left, fill bounce camera-right, hair light, 5500K, even soft shadow
- editorial-fashion: Single hard key with gel or grid, deep shadow, dramatic separation, 4500-5500K
- street-style: Available daylight with reflector fill, 5000K, candid feel
- outdoor-natural: Golden hour backlight with reflector fill, 4500K mixed warm, atmospheric haze
- home-lifestyle: Window light with practical lamp fill, 3500K warm, soft cozy
- closeup-detail: Beauty dish or soft directional, narrow DoF, sharp catchlight, 5500K
- runway-style: Strong key with rim, slight haze, dramatic falloff, 5000K
- flat-lay-on-body: Even soft top-down lighting, minimal shadow, 5000K neutral

### Conceptual and stylized product
- levitating-suspended: Single soft key from above-front 45°, deep shadow background, soft contact shadow under floating product, 5000K
- splash-frozen-motion: Hard strobe with high-speed freeze, multiple flashes for splash separation, 5500K daylight, sharp shadow definition
- abstract-cgi-render: Multi-source studio lighting with controlled bounce, even key with rim separation, 5500K clean daylight, mirror-like reflections
- sculptural-arrangement: Single dramatic key with grid, deep architectural shadow, 5000K, monumental feel
- liquid-pour-suspension: Hard backlight to define liquid, rim separation, 5000K, freeze every droplet sharp
- broken-deconstructed: Even soft top-down lighting, minimal shadow, 5500K, clinical clarity
- floating-elements: Soft directional key with multiple subtle fills, 4500K, ethereal feel
- surreal-environment: Lighting motivated by impossible source — glow from inside subject, light from below, refracted color through liquid, 5000K cool
- geometric-pedestal: Single hard key with strong shadow, museum-style precision, 5000K
- chrome-liquid-metal: Soft dome lighting for clean reflection control, 5500K daylight, sharp specular highlights

### Lighting by brand tier (banners and hero images)
- Premium / luxury: hard rim light against deep shadow, single dominant source
- Approachable / DTC: soft window light, warm ambient, low contrast
- Energetic / bold: strong directional, saturated color, dramatic shadows
- Calm / wellness: diffused overcast, even tones, atmospheric haze
- Editorial / fashion: golden hour, anamorphic flare, cinematic depth

### Studio packshot looks
- clean-studio: seamless white-to-light-grey gradient, soft frontal key, minimal shadow
- dramatic-studio: hard rim light, deep shadows, single light source, moody backdrop
- minimal-design: pastel or single-color seamless, geometric simplicity, high negative space
- etsy-handmade: warm natural light, linen / wood / stone surfaces, organic shadow
- luxury-editorial: polished surface reflections, marble or velvet, jewel-tone backdrop
- vibrant-color: saturated solid background, bold color blocking, modern flat composition

## Supplement: camera angles and framing

Added to the verbatim lists above; standard photographic terms, not from the source dump.

**Camera position (always specify for products):**
- `eye-level straight-on` — front-facing packshot, label fully readable
- `slight hero low angle` — product feels dominant; keep perspective realistic
- `three-quarter view at 30-45° elevation` — the default catalog angle; shows front and one side
- `profile view` — silhouette check, shows shape and thickness
- `top-down 90° overhead` — flat lays, ingredient layouts
- `45° elevated` — tabletop lifestyle, food
- `dramatic low angle` — monumental, powerful; wide lens exaggerates, so prefer 50-85mm for products
- Avoid a tilted horizon (Dutch angle) on product stills unless asked for; it reads as an error.

**Framing and composition:**
- `extreme close-up / macro detail` — texture, label, cap
- `close-up` — product fills 60-70% of frame
- `medium shot` — product with immediate context
- `wide environmental shot` — product small, scene tells the story
- `rule of thirds placement` — subject on a third line, eye room toward the open side
- `centered symmetry` — formal, editorial, architectural
- `negative space` — calm area for a headline; describe it as part of the scene (sky, blurred wall, surface), never as an empty rectangle
- `leading lines` — surfaces, edges or shadows that direct the eye to the product
- `foreground depth element` — out-of-focus leaf, fabric edge, glass edge for layered depth

## Supplement: film and photographic looks

Optional looks; use one per prompt and only when the brief asks for a mood. Name the look by its visible properties, not just the stock name.

- `35mm film grain, soft halation around highlights, slightly lifted blacks` — nostalgic, documentary
- `warm pastel skin tones, gentle contrast, fine grain` — portrait-film look, lifestyle and beauty
- `saturated punchy color, strong blues and reds, fine grain` — vivid slide-film look, travel, outdoor
- `cool muted greens, soft contrast, airy highlights` — fashion-film look
- `high-contrast black-and-white with visible grain` — editorial, heritage
- `tungsten-balanced night look, red halation glow around lights` — cinematic night
- `medium-format clarity, smooth tonal transitions, deep detail` — high-end commercial still
- `clean digital commercial rendering, tack-sharp, neutral color` — e-commerce, catalog
- `anamorphic flare and oval bokeh` — cinematic banner (see also golden hour backlight)

## Supplement: color grading

- `neutral true-to-product color` — packshots where the brand color must be accurate
- `warm highlights with cool shadows (split-toned)` — cinematic lifestyle
- `teal-and-orange contrast` — action, outdoor, tech (use sparingly; can shift product color)
- `lifted matte blacks, desaturated pastel` — soft editorial, Pinterest-friendly
- `high-key: bright, low contrast, airy` — clean beauty, wellness
- `low-key: dark, single light, deep shadows` — luxury, fragrance, drama
- `bleach-bypass: reduced saturation, high contrast` — gritty, sporty
- `monochromatic palette with one saturated accent` — graphic, scroll-stopping ads
- Rule: for any product that must match a real item, ask for faithful product colors and apply the grade to the environment, not the product.
