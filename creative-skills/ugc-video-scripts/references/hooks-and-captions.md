## 1. Word budget and truth contract

Word density per clip (natural pace ~2.4-2.7 words/second, never rushed; energy comes from expression, not speed):
| clip length | talking / unboxing / try-on / tutorial | website tour (hard ceiling ~40 words per 15s) |
|---|---|---|
| <= 10s | ~12-20 words | 22-26 |
| 11-12s | ~20-28 | 28-33 |
| 13-15s | ~28-35 | 35-40 |
Sum across clips, then split into one segment per clip. Echoes and repeats count toward the budget and carry no information, cut them first. Product VO (off-screen) uses the same budget: ~12-20 words for <= 10s, 20-28 for 11-12s, 28-35 for 13-15s, split into one segment per clip and four beat-sized phrases per segment. A sparse script is legal for quiet process-led videos (<= 20 words per 15s clip, sound carries the clip).

Truth contract (always, including when the allowlist is empty):
- `approved_claims` (exact strings the user supplied) is the complete allowlist of product claims. Use each verbatim. Never strengthen, paraphrase, combine, quantify or derive another claim.
- With no allowlist, write claim-free copy about visible materials, controls, application, packaging and observable mechanics. No invented numbers, times, prices, percentages, purchase or usage counts, rankings, availability, outcomes or comparisons.
- A generated creator is a host or demonstrator, never a real customer. Do not invent purchase, ownership, use, results, before/after outcomes, ratings, reviews, social proof, relationships or lived experience. First-person experience is allowed only when the real person supplied the exact script and confirmed it is their own experience.
- Product-present output is a brand demo, creator concept or sponsored creative, not an organic customer review. Add an appropriate ad/sponsorship disclosure to the post package.
- Quoted lines and pattern examples below are structural examples, never factual source material.
- Productless mode is legal: a creator tells a supplied story or routine; never invent a product, brand, product entrance or sales CTA, and end on the human resolution.

## 2. Voice archetype and persona sentence

Every video gets ONE performance persona that colours HOW the creator talks. Resolve the register first (NATURAL by default; HYPED or CALM only on an explicit signal), then pick from that tier.
- NATURAL tier (default): a warm, genuine, engaged creator: girl-next-door / easy-going relatable / dry-witty. Lively and real, one honest human-scale peak (a real grin, a delighted "oh"), never squealing or screaming.
- HYPED tier (explicit signal only): 2000s It-Girl (stretched vowels, squeals on the reveal), Hype Beast (explosive bursts), Drama Queen.
- CALM tier (explicit signal only): Deadpan Contrast (flat, one micro-crack at the peak), quiet Gossip Whisperer, Southern Sweetheart (soft warm).

Rule: under the NATURAL default the persona sentence carries NO energy words (never squeal, scream, stretched vowels, explosive). Energy comes from the register; the persona adds only attitude, melody and cadence, never volume. Write the persona as ONE sentence and carry it verbatim into the creator prompt and every shot prompt, or it drifts. Archetype and accent stack independently.

Accent and quirk are opt-in. If the brief names an identifiable origin for the creator, or asks for "weird / viral" character energy, offer accent + one quirk inside the single intake question. Never add an accent the user did not approve. Default is neutral with no quirk. Example persona line: "a young Korean it-girl, English is her second language and you hear it in every sentence".

## 3. Story shapes (the spine) and review skeletons

Story mode is the default: ONE scenario spans the whole video, with situational stakes framed without claiming a generated creator really lived them. If a product is present it enters as a SUPPORTING ACTOR at 40-60% of total runtime (stakes in the hook, one "but then" twist opening the mid-arc, the CTA riding INSIDE the closer's resolution, never as an outro beat). The story must survive with the product deleted. Brainstorm at least 3 candidate shapes, choose one; combine two only if it deepens the gap.

- S1 Demonstration Discovery (problem appears, feature is demonstrated)
- S2 Mechanism Loop (open on an observable action, then show how it works)
- S3 Alternate Use (show a second visible use without claiming personal ownership)
- S4 Guided Comparison (compare only visible mechanics or exact allowlisted claims)
- S5 Behind-the-Concept (explain the creative or product setup without a testimonial)
- S6 Interrupted Storytime (a story about something else; the product interrupts, the story never quite finishes)
- S7 GRWM With Stakes (getting ready FOR a charged thing; the stakes carry it)
- S8 New-Place Mini-Vlog
- S9 Day-in-the-Life With a Twist
- S10 Product Reveal (clearly staged reveal, no claimed first reaction)
- S11 POV Frame (talks TO the viewer as a character)
- S12 Process Win (real work, one small win; native for B2B and small business)
- S13 FAQ Reply (answer a product question with observable evidence)
- S14 Green-Screen Commentary (talk over on-screen evidence)
- S15 Feature Checklist (show a set of observable attributes)
- S16 Host Question (one direct question answered by the demonstrator)
- S17 Baseline-to-Demo (show an observable setup change without invented results)
- S18 Silent Flex (zero spoken words)
- S19 ASMR (<= 15 whispered words, the SFX line becomes the script, each trigger named and timed, macro framing, restate "no on-screen words of any kind" because models love baking karaoke subtitles into ASMR)

Every shape carries exactly ONE "but then" twist (the mid-arc peak). No twist = a flat anecdote; two will not fit in 15s.

Plain review / demo mode (only on an explicit "review it / plain demo" ask): convert the request into a truthful demonstration, not a testimonial. One compact skeleton: PAS (Problem, Agitate, Solve), BAB (Before, After, Bridge), Hook-Story-Offer, Us-vs-Them (against a lazy pricey default), or Demonstration ("watch this": the product does something visible in ~5s). Two or three spoken lines; the FIRST line carries one observable detail or exact allowlisted claim. Never invent a before/after result.

Optional registers: Quiet process-led (soft half-thoughts, product enters as one honest routine step, skincare/beauty routines start bare-faced; tell the shot prompts the register in ONE plain staging sentence). Silent / ASMR (S18 / S19, best for products with satisfying physical feedback; the absence of a synthetic voice is itself the anti-slop move; the CTA lives only in the post package). Music is opt-in; never invent music uninvited.

## 4. Hooks

The first 1-2 seconds decide everything. Pick ONE pattern per video, never blend. The pattern decides what KIND of moment the opener is; the first-word rule governs its words. H2/H5/H7 are text-led (the line embodies them); H1/H3/H4 are visual-led (the line lands mid-event and the staging carries it). Write the chosen pattern into the first shot prompt as ONE plain visual staging sentence, never a pattern code.

- **H1 Impact Action:** frame one is something physical mid-peak (box mid-rip, product mid-catch, mid-stumble); the first word lands DURING the action.
- **H2 Mid-Sentence Confession:** opens on word four of a sentence, as if the viewer walked in on it; confessional volume, close to the lens.
- **H3 Pattern Interrupt:** a normal setting with one thing deeply wrong, delivered with total normalcy; the wrongness is visual, the voice ignores it.
- **H4 Freeze-Reaction:** frame one is the face already in full reaction, locked on something; a performed hold (<= 0.7s), THEN the first line. The one legal delay of the first word.
- **H5 Hostile Open:** the first line is a challenge or accusation aimed at the viewer; slight lean-in, finger already pointing at the lens.
- **H6 Quirk-First:** the quirk IS the literal first event, before any context. Only when a quirk was opted into.
- **H7 Mechanism-First:** an observable product action is already underway in frame one; the first line names that visible action or repeats an exact allowlisted claim. Never imply a personal outcome, transformation or before/after.
- **H8 Product Cold Open** (advanced, story mode, needs a product image): shot 1 is clearly staged product-only footage (rougher light, slight compression, no face); ONE hard cut into shot 2 where the creator is mid-reaction with the product already in hand and the first line lands on the cut. The creator enters one shot later. Never frame it as found/reposted third-party footage and never add social proof.

Hook variants for a single-clip talking review (slot 1): (n) Natural engaged hook (DEFAULT): already mid-moment, a genuine human-scale reaction landing on the product/action from frame 1, raised brows with a grin already breaking, a real "wait, what?" lean-in; (b) Peak-shock skit hook (explicit hype signal only); (a) Classic talking-head hook (warm-but-restrained: selfie pose, product visible in one hand at arm's length, talking direct-to-lens); (c) Mid-action straight-in hook (already mid-bite / mid-sip / mid-sentence at frame start); (d) Sustained-passive hook (calm briefs: neutral face, half-lidded gaze, deliberate stillness; a dramatic deadpan stare with no smile pairs with the deadpan-then-crack pattern).

First-word constraint (positional, every segment): the literal FIRST WORD must be hook content. Never `OK / Okay / Okay so / Alright / Alright so / So / Yeah so / Right so / Um / Well / Like / Wait / Wait what / Hold on` (they read as recording warm-up), and never `OMG / Oh my god you guys / Hey guys / Guys / So basically / Story time / Stop scrolling / You NEED this / Let me tell you about`. They are fine mid-sentence. Friction beats enthusiasm ("I almost returned this.") and landing mid-action on Step 1 with no verbal opener is also legal. Bracketed sounds at the very start do not count as words.

### Hook angles for ad / website hooks (<= 8 words, <= ~2 seconds, one clause, with a hook TOKEN)
A hook token is a number, a price, a percentage, a unit of time, a contrast, or a named result. Without a token it is not a valid hook ("these are amazing" fails). No greeting, no pointing intro ("This is X", "Here's", "Meet", "I found", "I want to show", "Let me show", "Say hello to"). The hook may reference the brand only inside a claim; naming the site as the thing you use is the FIRST BODY beat.
| Angle | Use when | Template |
|---|---|---|
| Price contrast | cheaper than it looks | "$65 sunglasses that look like $300." |
| Secret / reveal | premium look for little money | "Everyone thinks these are designer." |
| Social-proof number | lots of sales / reviews | "3 million people bought these." |
| "Stop X" conflict | a category people overpay in | "Stop overpaying for sunglasses." |
| Transformation / result | SaaS, service, earnings | "This site saved me 20 hours a week." |
| Speed | fast result / delivery | "Found my perfect pair in 60 seconds." |
Hook example: "Sold out? Not on adidas." (claim + token). First body beat: "so I went straight to the adidas site..." Self-check before generating: <= 8 words; a token in the first breath; no banned opener; does not just restate what the picture already shows. Keep claims plausible: a bold but believable claim, never scammy. When the allowlist rule is active, tokens must come from the allowlist or from observable facts, not from invented numbers.

Hook bank by pattern (EN structural examples; swap in real, allowed details):
- H1: "[box rips] That was NOT supposed to happen." / "[catches the bottle] Close call, that one almost ended it."
- H2: "...and that's why the lid stays in my bag now." / "...third time this week, so I fixed it."
- H3: normal kitchen, a shoe in the sink, voice calm: "Two minutes and it's done."
- H4: (freeze, eyes locked on the product, 0.5s) "Who designed this latch?"
- H5: "You're still doing this with a cloth?" / "Stop scrubbing that by hand."
- H7: (thumb flicks the lid open) "One-handed, that's the whole trick."
Polish adaptations (my own, check against the allowlist and brand tone before use): H2 "...i dlatego ta pokrywka już nie wraca do torebki." / H5 "Nadal czyścisz to szmatką?" / H7 (kciuk odskakuje klapkę) "Jedną ręką, na tym polega cały myk." / Price contrast "Okulary za 99 zł, które wyglądają jak za 400." / Speed "Znalazłem idealną parę w 60 sekund." / Stop X "Przestań przepłacać za okulary." / Transformation "Ta strona oszczędza mi 20 godzin tygodniowo." (only if true and supplied).

## 5. Anti-slop pass and spoken texture

Run on every line.
- Banned anywhere: "literally", "obsessed" (all forms, no exceptions), "game-changer", "holy grail", "changed my life", "hits different", "and honestly?", "you have to try this / see this", "you NEED this", "trust me on this", "I cannot recommend enough", "ten out of ten", "10/10", "100%", "1000%", and corporate words: elevate, seamless, effortless, leverage, revolutionary, game-changing, synergy, unlock, supercharge. Also no "This is X, not Y" constructions (an AI giveaway), no repeated "you guys".
- Generic praise without specifics is cut: "it's amazing", "it's incredible", "so good", "mind-blowing", "unreal", "out of this world". Replace with proof of behaviour or the specific change (`holds even on shag`, `I stopped renting a steamer`).
- Every claim carries ONE concrete (a number, a time, a place on the body, a named comparison): "Cut reporting from three hours to ten minutes" beats "saves time". When the allowlist rule is active, never add or substitute a concrete; use observable mechanics.
- Demonstration-first openers beat unsupported enthusiasm ("Here is the part that twists open.").
- At most ONE peak reaction per clip in the line (the video prompt may stage up to two: the arc peak plus an optional product-motivated closer beat).
- Specific demonstrator language: scent `smells like jasmine and pepper`, `vanilla with a smoky finish`; texture `melts in instantly, no stickiness`, `goes on like silk`, `dries down in seconds`; context `the cap twists open with one hand`, `the texture spreads in one pass`. Demonstrators describe visible mechanics and immediate sensory qualities, not invented history.
- Spoken texture (write how people TALK): contractions always; 1-2 conversational connectors per monologue (`ok so`, `honestly`, `no joke`, `and get this`, `here's the thing`) but never as the first word of the hook; vary sentence length (clipped 2-4 word lines mixed with longer ones); ONE live imperfect beat (a micro self-correction, a thinking-out-loud aside, a reaction to yourself: "wait, that's actually insane"); varied pace WITHIN the take (quicker on throwaway lines, slow down to LAND the number, the price and the CTA).
- Performed dialogue: vowel-stretch on the peak word ("it's SO good", "saved me TWENTY hours"), 1-2 CAPS spikes per line max, one broken sentence at the peak ("it's... ok wait. LOOK."), a whisper-to-spike swing, a flat, spike, settle arc across the clip. Keep it human-scale. Emotion goes into the words, not into pauses (one engineered pause maximum, in the hook or the closer).
- Monologue self-check: sounds alive said out loud (not a read ad); contractions present; one connector; sentence length AND pace vary; the peak line performs the emotion.

## 6. Line banks

### Try-on: personal-want / arrival mini-story (board 1 opener; as a concept for a brand demo, only when the allowlist and truth contract permit)
Gender-neutral: "I have been eyeing this for actual months and the package finally landed" / "this has been sitting in my saved tab since the drop, and today is the day" / "you don't even understand how long I have been waiting for this one" / "I have been refreshing the tracker every hour for three days and it is finally here" / "I saw this the second it came out and I knew it was mine" / "this exact one has lived in my head rent-free since I first saw it" / "the second I saw the photos I started saving up, and here we are" / "this is the one I have been holding out for, no exaggeration" / "okay confession, I have been talking about this piece for weeks to anyone who would listen".
Female-coded: "I have wanted this bag / dress / top forever and my wallet finally let me" / "this has been on my Pinterest board for months" / "I told my mom about this piece, I told my best friend, I told the group chat, and here it is on me". Male-coded: "I have been waiting on this drop for actual months, no joke" / "told my brother about this piece weeks ago, told my boys, told everyone, and now it is here" / "been saving up specifically for this one, and the package just landed" / "I am not someone who hypes pieces up easily, but this one has been on my mind for a while".
These are first-person lived-experience lines: they are structural examples for a script the real person confirms as their own. For a generated creator, use the demonstrator versions (fit, drape, fabric, observable details).
Observation banks (fit, fabric, location): "the way it just settles on my shoulders", "it sits at my waist like it was cut for me", "the drape falls past my knees just right", "the fabric is heavier than the photos made it look", "this weave catches light at every angle", "the stitching, the seams, every detail is right", "silk is soft but holds its shape", "the way this outfit reads in this corner", "this fit and this kitchen are a whole moment", "the wind just caught the hem", "this rain is doing something to the fabric, the surface beads up", "the light out here makes the color pop". Home-reflect (settled, storyful): "been wearing this all day, honest verdict, I am keeping it on", "now that I have actually lived in this for a few hours, the cut still holds", "this is going in the rotation, end of conversation". Real creators describe sensations, observations and the moment, not abstract feelings. Every line gets a concrete (a place on the body, a time).

### Product voice-over (benefit-driven, off-screen)
Describe WHAT THE PRODUCT DOES: function (`the cordless lift handles the whole apartment on one charge`, `the suction holds even on shag`, `the trigger fires a controlled cone of mist`), use case (`goes in the school-bag side pocket`, `dries in seconds, no streaks`), sensory specifics (`the texture goes on like silk and disappears`, `the click is satisfying every single time`), audience/fit (`built for people who actually clean their own car`, `made for fine hair that hates volume`). In allowlist mode these are style examples only: use visible mechanics and exact allowlisted claims. No greetings, no on-camera dialogue, no bracketed reaction sounds, opens mid-thought straight into a benefit.

### Tutorial narration
Measured and calm, explains what the creator is physically doing: "Two pumps is plenty for the whole face." / "Press it in with the pads of your fingers, don't rub." / "Wait thirty seconds before the next step." (use real supplied instructions). Later clips open mid-thought: "Now I press the pump twice...", "Then I rub it in like this...". Reserve the last ~0.5-1s for `Link in bio.`, `Follow me.` or `Subscribe!` (pick ONE by available time; `Subscribe!` for <= 0.5s). Shorten the instructional line rather than dropping a step.

### Unboxing
Board 1 uses a caved-in confession or other specific reveal-compatible hook, a body-event reaction at the reveal, one turn, and a natural resolution; later clips continue mid-thought. Literal first word of every segment is hook content.

### Website tour
Hook (<= 8 words, token) then body (first body beat names the site; one concrete line per section while its card is up: "800 reviews and it's a 4.9", "checkout's one tap", "the plan's ten bucks"; body words describe the REAL card on screen, never promise UI the capture does not show) then closer (result, concrete, CTA inside it, while the creator acts with the device/product). Runtime split ~8-12% hook, ~70% body, ~15% closer. Skip footer/logo-strip filler: no line, no card. Write one beat per captured screenshot, tagged `{section_label, words}`, in card order. All data in the lines must come from the page or the allowlist.

## 7. Series mode (opt-in)

Triggered only by an explicit ask for a SERIES / "a week of content" / "N posts from one creator". A series is N SEPARATE posts, each its own full run, sharing one creator and a week arc (a multi-clip video is ONE stitched video, not a series). Output the arc TABLE first and get a nod before generating.
- Carry forward VERBATIM: persona sentence, voice archetype, accent delivery, quirk, wardrobe anchors, the same creator reference image.
- 7-post skeleton: 1 = S10 Product Reveal; 2 = S1/S8 (creator earns attention, product barely mentioned); 3 = S7 GRWM With Stakes (first soft sell); 4 = S4 Guided Comparison; 5 = S3 Alternate Use (rewatch fuel); 6 = S2 Mechanism Loop (strongest demonstrable CTA); 7 = S5 Behind-the-Concept / quirk payoff (community post).
- Product-pressure curve: light (1-2), selling (3-6), community (7). A series that sells in every post burns the account.
- Quirk escalation: identical in posts 1-4, NOTICED by the creator in post 5, paid off or subverted in post 7.

## 8. Captions and hook plate

On-video text is OFF by default for UGC; generation must never bake text. Add captions only when the user opts into Subtitles, Hook or Both (website tour: captions ON by default, "Both" unless told otherwise; tutorial: Step N labels are required, extra text is optional). For the full caption system see `caption-and-title-systems`; this section is the UGC-specific recipe.

Timing comes from the finished video's ACTUAL speech: transcribe the final audio and use the words as the clock; never planned beats or evenly spread words. Keep the authored script as the display text (brand spelling, every spoken word); the transcript supplies the clock only. Premiere Pro: transcribe, then fix the text from the script. Check three cues (start, middle, end): a constant offset = wrong audio source, growing drift = bad alignment. If there is no speech, deliver the clean video, never invent captions.

Look (pick one; an explicit request is already the answer):
- **UGC-natural (default for UGC):** sentence case, compact outlined white text, single line, <= 4 words per cue, stroke ~0.045 of the font size, no plate, no animation. Natural wording, original case.
- **Bold TikTok caps:** ALL-CAPS white, thick black stroke (or no outline with soft shadow), one fitted font size for the whole video, max 2 balanced lines, bottom-anchored.
- **Heavy impact caps** (Anton), **clean geometric caps** (Montserrat ExtraBold), **handwritten torn paper** (cream scrap, deckled edges, fibre grain, soft shadow, dark handwritten text; fairy-tale / storybook / handcrafted looks).
- **Clean:** slim white caps, thin black outline, tiny, bottom ~12%, no box.
Fonts used in the source: TikTok Sans Bold, Montserrat ExtraBold, Anton, Caveat, Patrick Hand, Permanent Marker. Verify Polish diacritics (ą ć ę ł ń ó ś ź ż) render in the chosen font; the source found that some handwritten fonts lack Cyrillic, so never assume glyph coverage, and never ship blank or tofu glyphs.
Size and limits: <= 5 words and <= ~32 characters per caption, never a multi-line block, never covering the subject; no animations, no emoji, no karaoke. Hold a caption until the next one appears while speech is continuous; let it die ~0.2-0.4s after its own speech across a real pause.

Safe zones (vertical 9:16): keep the top 10% and the bottom ~17% clear (platform UI), sides ~11% of the width. These margins do not establish subject clearance: protect the moving face (including mouth and chin), hands, product labels, website cards, any hook plate and tutorial Step labels throughout every cue. Prefer a low position; try the upper safe area if the low one collides; never cover a face to keep a fixed position. If neither fits, ask for a composition fix instead of shrinking text until unreadable. For a new shot, leave a COMPACT plausible text location; never reserve an empty half-screen.

Review loop: transcribe, verify words against the script, burn/place captions, then inspect at least two frames at cue midpoints (present, readable, inside the frame, matching the cue) and confirm the audio length matches the video. Keep the clean master untouched; every style change starts from the clean master.

Hook plate (optional): a headline of <= 6 words at the top, ending at the actual first body-word boundary. Place it in the clean composition first, treat it as protected existing text during speech-caption placement, and do not duplicate the spoken hook words under it. Hook-only output needs no speech captions. Reuse the website tour's `hook.txt` line for the plate.

Tutorial Step labels: exactly one `Step N – Heading` per step (English Title Case, en-dash, single spaces, heading 1-4 words), global numbering (board/clip J holds steps 4*(J-1)+1 to 4*J). Identical typography, size, colour and position on every step; top-center about 5-7% from the top edge, shifting every label to bottom-center if a face crosses the top zone (never mix positions). ONE line preferred, two lines max with the same wrap pattern throughout; high contrast (white with soft shadow, or dark charcoal on bright scenes). Font vibe by category: skincare/luxury beauty = editorial serif (Didot / Bodoni / Playfair); fragrance = refined serif or thin elegant sans (Optima / Trajan); colour cosmetics = modern sans or italic serif; tech = modern geometric sans (Inter / Helvetica Neue / SF Pro); fitness / supplements = bold condensed sans in ALL-CAPS (Oswald / Impact / League Gothic) with the heading in caps; food = warm rounded sans or handwritten script; coffee / artisan = warm serif or hand-lettered display; fashion / jewelry = minimal thin sans or thin serif; home / decor = light serif; outdoor = modern bold-ish sans; default = clean sans regular. Keep speech captions in the bottom safe zone and a top hook only if it visibly clears the step text. Set these in Premiere/AE/Figma as editable layers rather than baking them into generated frames; if you bake them with Nano Banana Pro, give each caption as an exact quoted string with identical typography in every frame and check for glitched letters.

Style vocabulary from the shorts-style presets (names only, no prompts in the source): Bold Urban, Green Contrast, Urban Serenity, Warm Glow, Yellow Frame, Monochrome Vibes, Claymation, Marker Scribble, Sticker Type, Blue Comic, Red Graffiti, Watercolor, Pencil Sketch, Desktop, Acid Green, Balloon Type. Use them as a menu of caption/graphic looks for a series, not as ready prompts.

## 9. Post package (chat-only, on request)

Caption text, 3-5 hashtags, a pinned comment, a loop note, and the sponsorship/ad disclosure that fits the platform and the market (for product-present creator output; also label AI-generated persona where the platform requires an AI-content label). Keep claims inside the allowlist. Never publish on the user's behalf.
