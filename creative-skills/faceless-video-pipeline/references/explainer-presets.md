## 1. How to use this list

The source catalog is a gallery of 22 named looks for narrated explainers; every entry was listed with a 9:16 default aspect (use 16:9 for long YouTube videos). Each is a LOOK only: any look works on any channel type (Explainer, History, Kids, Fairy Tale, Picture Story), and the channel type keeps the story grammar, cut pattern and tone.

Two kinds of entries:
- **Pinned** (12 of 22, including the stills entry): the formula, palette lock and motion slot are written out in `style-packs.md`. Paste them verbatim.
- **Not pinned** (10 of 22): the source holds only the name. Section 4 gives a STARTER formula drafted from the name. Treat it as a draft: generate a style sample with it, compare with the look you or the user actually want (attach a reference image if there is one), and edit the formula until the sample matches; then lock the edited text and paste it byte-identical in every prompt. Do not claim a draft reproduces any particular product's preset art.

Lock procedure for any entry: choose the look -> choose ONE accent (if the look has an `{ACCENT}` slot) -> write the 80 to 100-word formula -> generate the style key (sample vignette, no swatches, no text) -> attach it to every asset and shot.

Motion family decides the NEGATIVE: flat 2D looks keep `3D render` in the NEGATIVE with `simple limited animation on twos`; dimensional looks drop `3D render` and use `smooth simple handcrafted motion, subtle stop-motion feel` (or the look's own token). The photorealism ban always stays.

## 4. Ten looks without a pinned formula (starter formulas) + the stills entry

Each starter is about 60 to 90 words; extend to 80 to 100 with the specifics of your reference. All are non-photorealistic. "Family" tells you the motion token and the NEGATIVE rule.

**12. Whiteboard Doodle** (flat 2D). One-liner: marker drawings appearing on a clean whiteboard.
> hand-drawn whiteboard doodle explainer: black dry-erase marker line drawings with slightly uneven strokes on a clean off-white whiteboard surface, minimal spot colors in red and blue marker only, simple icons, arrows, boxes and stick figures, smudge ghosts of erased marks, no shading, no gradients, drawings that appear stroke by stroke, non-photorealistic.

**13. 3D Papercraft** (dimensional). One-liner: layered cut-paper scenes with real depth.
> 3D papercraft diorama: scenes built from layered colored card and paper with crisp cut edges and visible thickness, soft realistic drop shadows between layers, gentle top-down studio light, matte paper fibre texture, folded and curled paper details, shallow miniature depth, handcrafted stop-motion feel, non-photorealistic handcrafted paper materials only.

**14. Mixed Media** (flat/painterly mix). One-liner: collage of photo cutouts, paint and drawing.
> mixed-media collage illustration: torn paper and photo-texture cutouts combined with loose acrylic paint strokes, ink doodles and tape, layered on a textured paper background, bold limited palette with one contrasting accent, visible brush marks and rough edges, slightly jittery cut-and-paste motion, non-photorealistic illustrated collage.

**15. Low Poly** (dimensional). One-liner: faceted polygon 3D worlds.
> low-poly 3D render: faceted flat-shaded polygon characters and landscapes with visible triangular facets, a restrained gradient palette of 4 to 6 harmonious colors, soft ambient light with crisp facet shading, simple geometric shapes, clean minimal backgrounds, smooth slow rotating camera moves, non-photorealistic.

**16. 2D Illustrator** (flat/painterly 2D). One-liner: polished editorial illustration.
> polished 2D editorial illustration: clean confident vector-like shapes with subtle texture and grain, balanced limited palette with harmonious tones, expressive stylized characters with simple faces, thoughtful composition with generous negative space, soft flat lighting with gentle shape shadows, restrained parallax motion, non-photorealistic illustrated look.

**17. Pixel Art** (flat 2D). One-liner: retro game sprites and scenes.
> retro pixel art: crisp square pixels on a low-resolution grid, limited 16-color palette, dithered shading, chunky sprite characters with simple animation frames, side-view and top-down game-like scenes, no anti-aliasing, no smooth gradients, frame-by-frame sprite animation at a low frame rate, non-photorealistic.

**18. Claymotion** (dimensional). One-liner: handmade clay stop-motion.
> claymation stop-motion look: hand-modeled plasticine clay characters and sets with visible fingerprints and soft imperfections, matte colorful clay surfaces, warm practical miniature lighting with soft shadows, shallow tilt-shift depth, slightly jittery stop-motion movement at a low frame rate, handmade tabletop sets, non-photorealistic.

**19. Isometric Flat Vector** (flat 2D). One-liner: tidy isometric diagrams.
> isometric flat vector illustration: clean 30-degree isometric scenes built from simple geometric blocks, solid flat color fills with only subtle tone-on-tone side shading, no outlines, crisp edges, a cohesive palette of 5 to 6 colors, tiny stylized figures for scale, plain light background, smooth diagrammatic motion, non-photorealistic.

**20. 3D Mix** (dimensional). One-liner: 3D elements mixed with flat graphics.
> 3D mix motion design: glossy smooth 3D objects and characters combined with flat 2D graphic elements and bold shapes, vibrant gradient-free color blocking with one hero color, soft studio light and gentle reflections, playful depth and layered parallax, snappy eased motion, non-photorealistic stylized render.

**21. Fluffy Toy** (dimensional). One-liner: plush stitched toys.
> fluffy plush toy world: characters and objects made of soft fuzzy fabric with visible fibre texture and stitched seams, button eyes, rounded squishy proportions, pastel and warm colors, soft diffused daylight with gentle shadows, cozy felt and fabric sets, bouncy cuddly movement, non-photorealistic handmade toy look.

**22. Paper collage** (flat 2D, stills). One-liner: the narrated-stills papercraft look. In the source catalog this entry was the stills-format card; treat it as Picture Story with the Flat 2D Papercraft formula in `style-packs.md` section 8, used verbatim (it is pinned there). Not a motion-clip look.

Notes:
- Entries 12 to 21 are drafts. Entry 22 and entries 1 to 11 are pinned.
- A look with a plain solid white or cream backdrop is easy for generators to hold; a look that depends on fine texture (clay fingerprints, plush fibre) drifts more, so keep prompts short and repeat the formula exactly.
- Real people on screen: describe by silhouette and build, never by name. Names belong to the narration.
