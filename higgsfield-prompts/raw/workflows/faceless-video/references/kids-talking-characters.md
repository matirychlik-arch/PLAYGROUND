# Kids · Talking Characters (`talking_characters: true`)

Read this ONLY when the run is a Kids video whose sound alternates between the narrator and
the cast (Round 1c answer "narrator + talking characters", or the prompt says the characters
talk). Every other run can ignore this file.

A Kids video where the storytelling ALTERNATES between the narrator and the cast:
**odd blocks are NARRATION** (external narrator, mouths closed — GOLDEN RULE 7 in full)
and **even blocks are DIALOGUE** (the characters speak their own lines, lips and all,
generated by `minimax_h3`). Duration is free, exactly like any other Kids video —
`blocks = ceil(requested seconds / 10)`, and the alternation just runs across however
many blocks there are (3 blocks → narration, dialogue, narration).

**The flag is not decoration: when it is true the WHOLE script is authored for it** —
block kinds, word budgets and prompts all change. When it is false, write the stable
build every other run uses and do not sprinkle character lines into it.

- **It is ONE story, chronologically bound.** Every dialogue block is CAUSED by the
  narration block before it and sets up the one after: narrator says the bee's tummy
  hurts → the bee says it out loud and the butterfly promises a doctor → narrator flies
  the butterfly across the meadow → the butterfly reaches the doctor. A dialogue block
  that could be swapped with another is a broken script: no character may know something
  the story has not shown yet (the doctor cannot appear before anyone called her).
- **Word budgets differ by block kind.** Narration keeps the Kids band (17–21 words per
  10s). Dialogue blocks are a little SHORTER — **16–22 words total across the block**, because two
  speakers need turn-taking gaps (measured 2026-07-29: at 16–26 the gaps grew into real
  dead air, so the floor went up) and kid dialogue lands harder when it is spare
  ("Ohhh, my tummy hurts so much!" / "Don't worry — I'll find a doctor, fast!").
  `validate_motion_script.py` enforces both bands off `block_kind`.
- **At most TWO speaking characters in one dialogue block**, named in the block, and each
  gets ONE turn (two at most). More than two voices in ten seconds reads as noise.
- **Voices come from `minimax_h3` itself, no voice swap.** That means a character's timbre
  MAY drift between dialogue blocks; it is the accepted trade-off for real dialogue inside
  a block. Keep every other identity anchor rock solid (same character design, same
  palette) so the eye carries what the ear cannot.
- **Audio per block:** narration blocks get their take the Phase-5 way as usual;
  dialogue blocks use the CLIP'S OWN audio — extract it at full length, normalize to
  −16 LUFS and hand it to the assembler as that block's voice file, never re-centred, so
  the lips stay locked. Subtitles then work unchanged: the per-block caption path reads
  each block's voice file, whichever way it was produced.
- **NOT compatible with SONG MODE inside one video** — a sung song replaces the narrator
  entirely, so there is nothing to alternate with. Across videos of the same channel the
  choice is free: video #1 can be a song and video #2 a dialogue run. `channel_dna`
  records `talking_characters` as INFORMATION about that video, never as a lock on the
  next one.
- **Everything else is normal Kids:** 4-cut blocks, the interplay, the default music bed,
  the same styles and the same assets.

**Editorial Motion Graphics is the flagship house style** — the default for BOTH
History and Explainer, pinned in `references/style-editorial-collage.md` (STYLE
FORMULA, palette lock, {MOTION} mapping, asset guidance) — no preset id, no
picker needed. Alternates stay one chip away: **Paper Diorama** (History's named
alternate — geopolitics/money/power or a "cinematic" ask;
`references/style-paper-diorama.md`), **3D Papercraft** (History) via the preset
gallery, and on Explainer the second MAIN direction **Stickman Cartoon** —
described GENERICALLY in every prompt ("crude paint-program webcomic: thin wobbly
black outlines, flat solid fills, egg-head dot-eye stick figures, plain
flat-color backgrounds") and **never naming a real comic/brand/IP**.
